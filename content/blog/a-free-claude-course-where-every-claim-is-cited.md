Most "learn AI" courses just ask you to trust them. [Bilko Academy](https://bilko.run/projects/academy/) shows its work instead. Tap a claim in a lesson and a card pops up with the exact line it came from. Get a quiz question wrong and you still get a real explanation, not a plain red X.

## Every claim has a source you can check

Open any lesson and you'll see small citation marks next to the claims that need backup. Tap one and a card opens with the source's name and the exact quote it came from. Scroll to the end of the lesson and you get a full Sources list, with a date next to each entry. One lesson's source reads "Claude Code — Overview — retrieved 2026-06-10," so you know someone checked the page, not just linked to it.

This matters because a course that teaches you to use Claude well should hold itself to the same bar. If a claim can't point to its source, it doesn't belong in the lesson.

![A lesson page with an inline citation marker highlighted in the body text](/blog-images/a-free-claude-course-where-every-claim-is-cited/academy-lesson.jpg "Each citation mark opens the exact quote it came from, right where the claim appears.")

## Nothing you type ever leaves your browser

An older version of Academy let you type your own prompt and send it straight to Claude from inside a lesson. The current version removed that. Every quiz question now ships its options pre-written, and the wrong options carry an explanation too, so picking the wrong one still teaches you something.

Your progress (which lessons you've passed, your points, your attempts) lives in your browser first. Sign in with your bilko.run account and it follows you to another device, merging instead of overwriting if you worked from two devices at once. Signing in is never required to take a lesson or a quiz.

![An end-of-lesson quiz question with a Sources list and a retrieved date below it](/blog-images/a-free-claude-course-where-every-claim-is-cited/academy-quiz.jpg "The quiz sits right after a dated source list, so you can check a claim before you're tested on it.")

## 13 lessons, free, no account needed to start

The course is built for people with no coding background and no AI experience. Open [bilko.run/projects/academy](https://bilko.run/projects/academy/) and you see the whole shape first. It's 13 lessons, about an hour and a half total, across 5 chapters. It shows zero lessons passed and zero points, because nothing sits behind a signup. Click "Start the course" and you're reading lesson one.

The five chapters run Meet Claude, Working in claude.ai, and Prompting. The last one covers trust and safety. It ends on a lesson that teaches Claude Code from zero, for a reader who has never opened a terminal.

![Bilko Academy course landing page showing 13 lessons, 1h 31m, 5 chapters, and a Start the course button](/blog-images/a-free-claude-course-where-every-claim-is-cited/academy-home.jpg "The course shows its full shape, free lessons and progress, before you sign in or commit to anything.")

Academy used to be a 20-lesson course about AI chatbots in general. This summer it narrowed to just being a Claude user. Since then it got simpler again: fewer moving parts, every exercise pre-written, every wrong answer explained. The next stop for a reader who finishes it is the Claude Code lesson at the end of chapter 5. It's built for someone who has never typed a command before.
